Curator's Note & Mission Rationale

Although born in G'uzor, many of my family relatives live in Shakhrisabz, and my grandfather guided me through these monumental landmarks since my early childhood. When applying for the Wikimedia grant, our team had options to visit well-trodden centers like Tashkent or Samarkand. I passionately advocated for Shakhrisabz: despite being an inscribed UNESCO World Heritage site, our research showed a severe digital knowledge gap—less than 6% of local historical landmarks were adequately documented online with high-quality media. This initiative empowered 20 young Uzbek creators to bridge that gap through open-access digital curation.

Innovation & Ethics

AI-Driven Heritage Restoration

As a Machine Learning student at Najot Ta'lim, I explore how Computer Vision and generative models can preserve crumbling Silk Road architecture without physical intervention.

By training diffusion models on surviving Timurid geometric tiles from Ak-Saray, youth conservators can mathematically reconstruct eroded architectural segments with cultural fidelity.

  • Non-invasive 3D reconstruction of eroded Ak-Saray majolica
  • Curating localized datasets to prevent algorithmic cultural bias
  • Decentralized open data governance (Wikimedia CC BY-SA standards)

Vision for 2026 IYF

At Changsha and Nanjing, I look forward to establishing international youth guidelines for open-source AI in Silk Road cultural preservation.
