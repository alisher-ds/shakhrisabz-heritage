01. Visual Work & Documentary

Shakhrisabz: Documenting 2,700 Years of Silk Road Heritage

A youth-led field expedition funded by the Wikimedia Foundation ($2,000 grant). Our 20-member team documented ancient Timurid monuments, sacred inscriptions, folk craftsmanship, and rural memory, uploading over 500 high-resolution assets to Wikimedia Commons.

“Although born in G'uzor, many of my relatives live in Shakhrisabz, and my grandfather guided me through these monumental landmarks since childhood. When our team applied for the Wikimedia grant, we had options to document heavily visited cities like Tashkent or Samarkand. I passionately chose Shakhrisabz because despite being an inscribed UNESCO World Heritage city, research showed that fewer than 6% of our historic settlements had adequate online representation. As Organization Manager and local on-the-ground guide, I led 20 peers through the city to ensure our Silk Road heritage is preserved openly for future generations.”

03. Technology & Heritage Ethics

AI-Driven Digital Heritage Restoration

Exploring how modern Machine Learning can serve cultural conservation while upholding ethical data stewardship along the Silk Road.

Computer Vision for Timurid Majolica Reconstruction

Having documented monumental Silk Road sites like the Ak-Saray Palace in Shakhrisabz—where centuries of erosion have damaged intricate 14th-century Timurid mosaic tiles—I see direct value in Computer Vision and generative models for non-invasive digital reconstruction. By training models on surviving geometric patterns, youth conservators can visualize accurate digital restorations without altering physical structures.

Crucially, this technical work must respect three ethical imperatives: curating localized datasets to prevent algorithmic cultural homogenization, ensuring open community ownership via Wikimedia, and maintaining human historical stewards in the decision loop.
